      How it works

      Three rooms, two of which you own.

      Every subreddit has a rule about vendors. We read it first and then act inside it. That is the whole method.

      Room one

      Say your name

      Some communities allow vendors to answer if they disclose. Here we reply under a labelled brand account and say who we are in the comment itself.

      Room two

      Correct only

      Some communities allow a factual correction but not a pitch. Here we fix wrong pricing, wrong limits, wrong feature claims. Nothing else.

      Room three

      Stay quiet

      Some communities forbid vendors outright. We do not post there. We do not use an unlabelled account to get around the rule.

      Owned ground

      The community sub

      A subreddit your brand runs, for the people who use the product. Questions, releases, workarounds, complaints. You moderate it. You keep it.

      Owned ground

      The citation sub

      A second subreddit built as a clean, factual record. Specs, limits, pricing, comparisons written straight. Made to be quoted correctly by an answer engine.

      The shape of the engagement

      Accounts
      Three. A client-owned brand account, a disclosed brand affiliate, and an aged disclosed operator account.
      Subreddits
      Two, both client-owned. One community sub, one citation sub.
      Comment volume
      Roughly 48 comments a month across the three accounts. Under four per account per week.
      Reporting
      Fortnightly. What was posted, where, and what happened to it.
      Engagement
      90 days, in three phases. Month to month after that.

      Why Reddit

      Answer engines read forums. Forums are where people say what they really think.

      When someone asks an AI assistant which tool to pick, the answer is assembled from pages that read like real people comparing real products. Reddit threads fit that shape better than a landing page does.

      Widely reported

      Reddit is cited heavily by AI answer engines

      We are not going to quote you a percentage. The published figures move and we cannot verify them. What is not in dispute is that forum threads show up in cited sources again and again.

      Why it holds

      A thread answers the question a buyer actually asked

      Your pricing page says what you charge. A thread says whether it was worth it. The second one is what gets summarised back to a buyer.

      The gap

      Wrong facts about you persist for years

      A comment from 2021 about a limit you removed still reads as current. Correcting it is allowed almost everywhere. Almost nobody does it.

      What we do not promise.

      Naming the limits is what makes the rest credible. If any of these are dealbreakers, we are the wrong people.

      We do not promise placement

      No one can guarantee a comment survives a moderator, a downvote brigade, or an automod rule. We can promise the work, not the reception.

      We do not promise a revenue number

      We will not model your pipeline from a comment count. If someone shows you that spreadsheet, ask them how they built it.

      We do not promise an AI engine will cite you

      Answer engines change what they read and how they weight it. We build the clean, quotable record. We do not control the retrieval.

      We do not post where vendors are banned

      If we cannot say who we work for in a room, we do not talk about the client in it. That rule costs us reach. We keep it anyway.

      We do not hide the accounts

      Every account we run for you is disclosed. You get the logins for the two subreddits and the brand account. They are yours at the end.

      Find out what is already being said. Then decide.

      Start with a short call or start with the 90-day programme. Either way, month to month. You keep the subreddits and the documentation.
